# ADR-047: Qobrix copy ownership is preserved and exceptions are manager-approved claims

**Status**: Accepted
**Date**: 2026-08-29
**Depends on**: ADR-044 (Qobrix surface partition + copy-on-write), ADR-046 (Qobrix broker → SSO identity map)
**Scope**: `matrix-sales-automation` App DB and its Qobrix copy-on-read paths

## Context

Qobrix applies the first ownership filter: an ordinary broker reads only records
owned by or assigned to their Qobrix account. A supervisor or Area Manager can
legitimately see a colleague's record, however. Previously, opening that record
and confirming the copy stamped the opener as the local owner. Leads silently
changed assignee, contacts became owned through authorship, and opportunities
could retain a provenance badge while App-DB RLS still followed the copier.

That is a managerial reassignment, not a technical copy. It must not happen
without an Area Manager decision, and a rejected request must remove the
requester's self-scope access.

## Decision

### D1 — Preserve the mapped Qobrix owner

The confirmed `qobrix_user_sso_map` binding is the canonical bridge from the
Qobrix owner/assignee to the SSO `sub` stored in MSA. On `owner_mismatch`, the
copied lead, contact, or opportunity keeps that mapped SSO subject as its local
assignee. If the Qobrix owner is not mapped, the assignee remains null; the
missing mapping never defaults ownership to the opener.

An `identity_mismatch` is different: the record belongs to the signed-in
Qobrix account, but the Qobrix and SSO emails differ. It remains a self-confirm
and does not enter the manager queue.

On 2026-08-29 management confirmed the four previously unconfirmed but
bindable broker matches (Anna Shah, Elena Pestova, Natalia Orlova, and Tatiana
Stromina). The assignee backfill then populated four existing opportunity rows;
unmatched Qobrix users remain unassigned rather than being guessed.

### D2 — Claims live on the copied row

Leads and contacts use `mirror_claim_state`, `mirror_claim_reason`,
`mirror_claimed_by`, and the resolved-by/time fields alongside their existing
Qobrix provenance columns. Opportunities use the same family with the `x_`
prefix to preserve the Qobrix-vanilla-inside model. A pending partial index
supports the manager queue.

The requester can work with a pending claim. Approval assigns the row to the
requester and records the manager decision. Rejection clears the claimant and
records the reason. A pending opportunity remains editable for notes and
activities, but its stage is locked until approval. Contracts have no
independent claim: their visibility inherits the parent opportunity claim.
Listings and projects remain catalog metadata and do not participate in this CRM
transfer flow.

### D3 — RLS follows source ownership and live claims

`can_read_claimed_row` preserves the existing scope behavior for ordinary rows.
For mismatch rows it grants self access to the mapped assignee, to the live
pending/approved claimant, and to broader team/global/admin scopes. A rejected
claim therefore removes the requester's self-scope access; `created_by` alone
does not keep it alive.

The copy opener still supplies `created_by` and `owner_team_id` as provenance.
`owner_team_id` intentionally continues to follow the opener for now because
the assignee's team is not present in the opener's JWT. Team scope therefore
remains a deliberate residual and is not treated as a reassignment.

### D4 — Manager workflow and messaging

Area Managers and higher scopes use `list_ownership_claims`,
`approve_ownership_claim`, and `reject_ownership_claim`. The UI exposes a
dedicated Ownership transfers queue, includes those requests in the sidebar
count, and notifies a mapped source owner when a claim is raised.

The copy dialog says **Request ownership** for `owner_mismatch` and **Cancel**
does nothing. It explicitly says that the Qobrix owner remains the owner until
the Area Manager decides. Opening a Qobrix record never copies, reassigns, or
raises a manager request without that deliberate choice. The
identity-mismatch dialog remains a self-confirm. Lead, contact, and opportunity
detail pages share one status banner describing pending, approved, and rejected
states, and all three copy gates use the same dialog.

### D4b — One four-step UX for every Qobrix-sourced CRM entity

Leads, opportunities, contacts, and contracts share the same client contract
(preview → ownership confirm → stage block while a claim is pending →
on-demand sync). Opening a Qobrix-only row never writes to the App DB; the
shared `QobrixRecordPreview` asks the broker to **Add to my …** first. The
`qobrix-materialize` Edge Function is the single front door (`preview` /
`copy` / `sync`) over an entity registry; `qobrix-crm-mirror` and
`backfill-opportunity-from-qobrix` remain thin writers behind it.

After materialize, **Sync from Qobrix** fills blanks and flags field
mismatches in polymorphic `qobrix_field_mismatches` (Keep local / Accept
Qobrix via `QobrixMismatchPanel`). Listings keep their queued remirror
overwrite semantics but reuse the same `QobrixSyncButton` and `QobrixMark`.
Contracts may be claimed independently; child contracts copied with an
opportunity inherit the parent's gate outcome.

While `mirror_claim_state` / `x_mirror_claim_state` is `pending`, the record
cannot advance (opportunity stage gate, lead qualify, and contact-claim
blocking of the linked deal). Copy in the pending banner says progression is
blocked — not "you can keep working meanwhile" without that constraint.

### D5 — Qobrix comments remain live, read-only history

MSA exposes Qobrix comments on the opportunity/lead preview and detail
surfaces, and on the contact timeline, through the app-specific
`qobrix-comments` Edge Function. It reads
`/{opportunities|contacts}/{id}/comments` using the caller's Qobrix session,
so Qobrix remains the authority for both visibility and content. MSA resolves
comment authors for display where possible, but never edits, mirrors, or
merges these entries into the App-DB `sales_entity_notes` sidecar. The latter
continues to represent private MSA notes only.

## Consequences

- A supervisor can copy a colleague's record without silently taking it.
- The source owner retains self-scope visibility while a request is pending.
- A rejected request has enforceable access consequences for its requester.
- Qobrix remains read-only; approval changes only the MSA copy.
- Existing self-claims are grandfathered as approved to avoid removing active
  work from brokers during rollout.
- Listings retain tenant-wide catalog visibility as defined by ADR-036.
